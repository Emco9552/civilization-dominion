# Create a Map-Based Civilization Domination Game

Use these uploaded files:

* `RLmap.png` — the main world and biome map.
* `CoolBorders.PNG` — reference for country borders and visual effects.
* `Border.png` — clearer reference for country borders.
* `Human Borders.png` — shows the human country outlined in red.

Use `RLmap.png` as the main visible map.

Country borders must follow the uploaded references. Do not invent countries, remove small countries or divide countries based on biome changes.

Biomes and political borders are separate.

# Main Goal

Create a civilization strategy and domination game.

The player should be able to:

* Select a country and species.
* Build and manage the country.
* Research technologies.
* Recruit armies.
* Use diplomacy and espionage.
* Talk to leaders and citizens.
* Conquer territory.
* Win through domination or another victory condition.

Keep the game manageable. Each feature should work, but it does not need to be extremely deep.

# Game Modes

## Standard Mode

Standard Mode should have faster progress, simpler management and easier wars.

## Realistic Mode

Realistic Mode should use the same systems but make them harder.

It should include slower progress, stronger war consequences, more expensive decisions and more importance placed on morale, stability and conversations.

# Player Leadership

The player becomes the leader of the selected country.

The leader title depends on the government, such as:

* King.
* President.
* Dictator.
* Emperor.

Governments should provide small advantages and disadvantages.

The player may change the government later, but it should cost resources and temporarily reduce stability.

# Interactive World Map

Recreate every country as a clickable region over `RLmap.png`.

Each country should:

* Highlight when hovered over.
* Be selectable.
* Display basic information.
* Use a transparent ownership colour.
* Change ownership after conquest.

Keep the biomes visible underneath the ownership colours.

Follow the border references carefully, especially on the large top-right continent.

# Regions

Countries may contain a small number of internal regions.

Regions can contain:

* Cities.
* Resources.
* Buildings.
* Armies.

Regions can be captured during wars.

Do not create too many regions.

# Countries and Species

Every country should have its own fictional species.

Each species needs:

* Name.
* Appearance.
* Language.
* Culture.
* Government.
* Technology style.
* Strengths.
* Weaknesses.
* Special ability.
* Short history.

When selecting a country, show this information with a `Play` button.

Descriptions can be short.

# Humanity

Humans control the large country marked in `Human Borders.png` on the top-right continent.

Humans should be one of the strongest playable civilizations.

Human strengths:

* Best intelligence.
* Fast research.
* Strong education and engineering.
* Good strategy and diplomacy.
* High adaptability.

Human weaknesses:

* Low physical strength.
* Low natural durability.
* Weak early melee units.
* Dependence on equipment.

Humans should become very powerful through technology.

They may reach advanced technology while other countries remain in older eras.

# Species Statistics

Use these statistics:

* Intelligence.
* Strength.
* Durability.
* Agility.
* Population Growth.
* Productivity.
* Diplomacy.
* Morale.
* Adaptability.

Every statistic should have one clear gameplay effect.

For example:

* Intelligence increases research.
* Strength improves melee units.
* Productivity improves resources and construction.
* Diplomacy improves agreements.
* Morale affects armies and stability.
* Adaptability reduces biome penalties.

# Technology System

Technology should not depend on calendar years.

Civilizations only advance by researching technologies.

Different civilizations may progress at very different speeds.

A technologically advanced country should have a major advantage over a less advanced one.

# Technology Eras

Use these eras:

1. Primitive Era.
2. Ancient Era.
3. Medieval Era.
4. Industrial Era.
5. Modern Era.
6. Information Era.
7. Futuristic Era.
8. Interplanetary Era.

# Unlocking Eras

The next era unlocks after researching approximately 75% of the technologies in the current era.

Example:

* The Medieval Era has 12 technologies.
* The player researches 9.
* The Industrial Era unlocks.

Old unfinished technologies should remain available.

Show era progress clearly:

`Medieval Era: 67%`

At 75%:

`Industrial Era unlocked`

# Technology Tree

Create a visual technology tree.

Use around 8–12 technologies in each era.

Main categories:

* Military.
* Economy.
* Education.
* Infrastructure.
* Medicine.
* Government.
* Energy.
* Space.

Each technology should show:

* Cost.
* Requirements.
* Effect.
* Era.

Technologies may unlock units, buildings or bonuses.

# Research Points

Technologies require research points.

Research points should mainly come from:

* Species intelligence.
* Population.
* Schools.
* Universities.
* Laboratories.

Humans should generate research points faster than other species.

The player researches one technology at a time.

# Technology Sharing

Countries may gain technology through:

* Trade.
* Alliances.
* Espionage.
* Capturing advanced regions.

Technology should not spread automatically.

# Resources

Use a small resource system:

* Food.
* Materials.
* Energy.
* Money.
* Research Points.
* Population.

Biomes should affect resource production.

For example, fertile areas produce more food and mountains produce more materials.

# Cities and Buildings

Countries should contain several cities.

Cities produce resources and contain buildings.

Main buildings:

* Houses.
* Farms.
* Mines.
* Factories.
* Schools.
* Universities.
* Laboratories.
* Hospitals.
* Military bases.
* Fortifications.
* Power plants.

Every building should have a clear effect.

Do not create a complicated city-building simulator.

# Economy

The economy should be based mainly on:

* Taxes.
* Resource production.
* Trade.
* Building and military costs.

In Realistic Mode, shortages and excessive spending should have stronger consequences.

# Armies

The player should be able to recruit armies.

Units depend on the current technology era.

Examples:

* Medieval armies use swords, bows and trebuchets.
* Industrial armies use rifles and artillery.
* Modern armies use tanks and aircraft.
* Futuristic armies use robots and energy weapons.

Keep unit statistics simple:

* Attack.
* Defence.
* Health.
* Speed.
* Morale.

# Warfare

The player can attack neighbouring regions.

Battle results should depend on:

* Army size.
* Technology.
* Species statistics.
* Morale.
* Terrain.
* Fortifications.

Advanced technology should usually defeat older technology, but numbers and defence should still matter.

# Conquest and Occupation

Regions can change ownership during war.

A country is defeated after losing most of its regions and its capital.

After victory, the player may:

* Annex the country.
* Create a subject country.
* Demand territory.
* Make peace.

Conquered regions should have reduced morale for some time.

Realistic Mode should make occupation harder.

# Diplomacy

Include these diplomatic actions:

* Improve relations.
* Trade.
* Alliance.
* Military access.
* Research agreement.
* Declare war.
* Make peace.
* Demand territory.
* Create a subject country.

AI countries should react based on their personality, strength and relationship with the player.

# Espionage

Add a simple espionage system.

Possible actions:

* Steal technology.
* Reveal armies.
* Sabotage production.
* Support unrest.

Spy actions should cost money and have a chance to fail.

Failure may damage diplomatic relations.

# Artificial Intelligence

Every non-player country should be controlled by AI.

The AI should be able to:

* Build.
* Research.
* Recruit armies.
* Defend itself.
* Attack enemies.
* Trade.
* Form alliances.
* Use basic espionage.

Countries may have simple personalities such as aggressive, peaceful, scientific or defensive.

# AI Conversations

Realistic Mode should include a conversation system.

The player can talk to:

* Their own city leader.
* A random citizen.
* A foreign leader.

The player should be able to type messages or choose suggested questions.

Characters should respond based on simple game information such as:

* Current wars.
* Resources.
* Morale.
* Relationships.
* Recent decisions.

Foreign leaders can discuss peace, trade, alliances, threats and territory.

Citizens can discuss shortages, safety, war and public morale.

The conversation system does not need complex human-level memory. It only needs to react to important current information.

# Promises

Important promises made during conversations should be stored.

Examples:

* Promise peace.
* Promise resources.
* Promise military support.
* Promise to return territory.

Breaking a promise should reduce trust and relations.

# Government and Country Customization

The player may change:

* Country name.
* Flag.
* Government.
* Official language.
* Capital.
* Leader title.

Every change should cost something.

Possible costs:

* Money.
* Morale.
* Stability.
* Political influence.

Small visual changes should be cheap.

Changing the government or language should be more expensive.

# Permanent Characteristics

The player cannot directly change:

* Species.
* Species appearance.
* Natural strengths.
* Natural weaknesses.
* Special abilities.
* Base statistics.
* Original geography.
* Biomes.
* Starting history.

Technology may reduce weaknesses without changing the original species.

For example, humans remain naturally weak but can use powered armour.

# Laws and Policies

Add a simple policy screen.

Possible policies:

* Tax level.
* Education spending.
* Military spending.
* Healthcare spending.
* Trade policy.
* Conscription.

Policies should provide clear bonuses and penalties.

Do not create a complex parliament system.

# Population

Track the country’s total population and basic morale.

Population affects:

* Resource production.
* Taxes.
* Research.
* Army recruitment.

Low food, high taxes and long wars should reduce morale.

# Events

Add simple random events.

Examples:

* Resource discovery.
* Food shortage.
* Scientific breakthrough.
* Protest.
* Natural disaster.
* Diplomatic offer.

Each event should have two or three choices with clear effects.

# Victory Conditions

Possible victories:

* Conquer the world.
* Control most countries.
* Become the most technologically advanced.
* Create a global alliance.
* Reach the Interplanetary Era first.

# User Interface

Use a clean futuristic style.

Main screens:

* Main menu.
* Mode selection.
* Country selection.
* World map.
* Country information.
* Technology tree.
* Resources and buildings.
* Military.
* Diplomacy.
* Espionage.
* Government.
* Conversations.

When hovering over a country, show:

* Country name.
* Species.
* Government.
* Technology era.
* Military power.
* Relationship.

# Visual Style

Keep the original biome appearance from `RLmap.png`.

Country ownership should use:

* Transparent colours.
* Clear borders.
* Hover highlighting.
* Selected-country effects.

Do not cover the biome map with solid country colours.

Use `CoolBorders.PNG` mainly as a visual reference.

# Important Border Rules

Follow the uploaded borders exactly.

Do not:

* Invent new borders.
* Remove small countries.
* Split countries because their biomes change.
* Make every island a separate country.

The human country must follow `Human Borders.png`.
